## Which failure mode is each FAIL?

Source: `control/results.json` / `control/results.md`, generated by `control/run_band_mechanism.py` at `ss` / -40 degC / 2.97 V, 3.3 V, 3.63 V on the same deck the rungs ran. A control is not corner evidence and substantiates no spec row (`sim/README.md`) -- the verdicts themselves come from the 81-point records above; the control only says what produced them.

The grid's one FAIL verdict (`resetn_ratio_min_in_dip` > 0.1) covers two physically different events:

- **NO DECISION** -- `POR_RAW` never asserts during the dip at all. This is DR-011's starved loop: `BIAS_OK` reads a false valid while the bank that biases it is driven off, so no threshold decision is ever taken.
- **LATE DECISION** -- `POR_RAW` does assert, well inside the dip, and the output chain -- starved by the same collapse -- does not finish propagating it before the dip window closes. `RESETn` then re-asserts on the recovery edge instead, which is the behaviour DR-011 already describes for out-of-envelope dips.

All times below are microseconds after the dip starts. *window closes* is `edge + 50 us dwell`, the end of the window `testbench/tb.json` measures over; `deglitch` is the interval from `POR_RAW` asserting to `PGDG` (the deglitched power-good) falling, i.e. `por_output_chain`'s dwell as actually realized at whatever bias current survived the collapse.

## Interpretation -- is DR-011's phase-relationship hypothesis confirmed?

`20260802-120940-3c3e728-boundary.md` offered a hypothesis, not a measurement: that the non-monotonicity reflects "the phase relationship between the collapsing loop and the measurement window" shifting near DR-011's own critical timing. The tables above let that be checked rather than merely repeated.

**The race is between two collapsing timers, not one.** Every FAIL in the table above is `RESETn` losing a race against *window closes*, and the deglitch ramp's own peak height (`NDG`/VDD, 0 = did not move, ~0.5-0.7 = the level the PASS rows above reliably cross) shows how close a near-boundary FAIL comes before the window shuts, rather than the ramp simply failing to start:

Two things follow from this table together with the event-timeline one above:

1. **The FAILs in the non-monotonic band are near-tangencies, not dead circuits.** The underlying state variable -- peak `NDG`/VDD, how high the deglitch ramp actually got -- declines *continuously* across the PASS-to-FAIL step (`ss`/3.3 V: 0.664 at 3.42 mV/us, 0.657 at 3.44, then 0.541 the instant it FAILs at 3.46), even though the verdict itself flips discretely, because a ramp that does not quite reach the trip point before the window closes produces the same binary FAIL as one that never moved at all. That is the near-tangency signature: a continuously-varying margin behind a discrete PASS/FAIL column, which is exactly the shape that makes a bisection-style search miscount monotonicity. This is the same shape DR-011's hypothesis predicted, now measured rather than inferred.
2. **`min V_sg in dip`'s sign is not itself the discriminator.** At 2.97 V it stays positive up to 3.613 mV/us and only inverts at 3.7 mV/us, well *after* that supply's own deglitch race is already lost (3.4795 mV/us, per the event-timeline table) -- a few mV of shaved overdrive, not a full shutoff, is already enough to stretch the deglitch dwell past the shrinking window. At 3.3 V and 3.63 V it is negative at *every* tested slew, including the healthiest PASS rows (3.351 mV/us: -84.0 mV / -113.5 mV) -- so a brief overdrive inversion during the dip is normal at those supplies and does not by itself predict the verdict either way. What tracks the verdict is the deglitch race margin in the event-timeline table, not `V_sg`'s sign at any single instant.

**Why the crossing point differs by supply.** `design/por_output_chain.md` documents the deglitch dwell as 1.86-4.58 us at nominal `IBIAS` and 3.61-8.88 us at half, and `spec/decision-records/DR-011-brownout-falling-slew-limit.md`'s own control C measures `por_output_chain` reaching valid-low in 3.70-7.30 us **down to zero `IBIAS`** -- but that control drives `POR_RAW` directly at a rail already settled at 1.0 V, decoupled from `bias_core`'s own collapse. The event-timeline table above shows every `deglitch` interval it could measure in this band running from several hundred microseconds up to 1048.1 us (`ss`/2.97 V at 3.4795 mV/us) -- one to two orders of magnitude past both documented ceilings where it resolves at all -- because in this regime the edge has not finished: at the near-boundary slews, `POR_RAW` asserts only 70-120 us into an edge that does not close its window for another 500-800 us, so the deglitch tail draws whatever current survives a collapse that is still in progress, not a settled one. That collapse trajectory -- how much overdrive is left at each instant, not merely at the moment `POR_RAW` trips -- is itself a function of the starting supply (a higher `vdd_val` buys a longer absolute edge duration for the same mV/us, and a different pre-dip operating point), which is the concrete reason the race's winner is not a function of slew rate alone: `ss_-40c_3.30v` and `ss_-40c_3.63v` cross this near-tangency at slightly different slews (see the ladder map above), while `ss_-40c_2.97v` -- whose absolute edge is shortest, leaving the least room in the race regardless -- fails first and does not recover in the tested ladder.

**Assessment: confirmed, and refined into a concrete mechanism.** The finding is consistent with DR-011's own framing -- a nonlinear relaxation dynamic near its own critical timing -- and the "phase relationship" language is now a specific, measured quantity: the margin between the deglitch dwell realized under in-progress bias collapse and the shrinking dip window, which the near-tangency data above shows crossing zero at slightly different slews for the three supply points rather than in lockstep. It is **not** a claim that `por_output_chain` is defective -- DR-011's control C and this record's own solver check (above) both stand -- only that the STATIC, decoupled-current condition those controls tested does not cover the DYNAMIC, still-falling-edge condition the non-monotonic band lives in. Neither this record nor its underlying data revisits or narrows `spec/target-spec.md#por-brownout` clause (c)'s ratified 3.40 mV/us bound: every PASS margin at or below 3.40 mV/us in the event-timeline table above (108.7-231.1 us, at 3.351 and 3.40 mV/us, the only two tested points at or below the ratified bound) sits well clear of this near-tangency band, which only opens at 3.46 mV/us and above.
